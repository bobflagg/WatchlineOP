# Q9: Owner Portfolio footprint and comparison

## Question

> Which lots, buildings and residential units are attributed to this building's Owner Portfolio, through which ownership signals, and how does that footprint differ from its Operational Network?

## Definition

An **Owner Portfolio** attributes buildings to one apparent owner from ownership signals only: a resolved shared identity, or a qualifying shared deed. Co-op and condo buildings are excluded by policy. It is a derived attribution, not a legal determination of beneficial ownership.

## Who asks and why

It gives the number tenants ask for first: how many buildings does this owner have.

## Required content of an answer

Per layer: run-scoped group id; member count in lots, buildings and units; boroughs; composition (identity, deed-only, deed-bridged) for portfolios, or whole-component or Louvain for networks; and the signals behind each member.

Between layers: members in one and not the other, with counts.

## Acceptable incomplete

- A building in no group, with the reason code (no qualifying linkage; excluded by policy; below the eligibility rule; outside source coverage; outside method input).
- Units missing for some lots: a partial total, with the number of lots missing a count.

## Failures to avoid

- A lot count reported as buildings.
- Layers mixed.
- A group presented as legal ownership, or a person anchor used as the owner.
- The run omitted.

## Caveats the answer must carry

- The eligibility rule counts member registration identities, not buildings: at least two are needed. A group is dropped when rental buildings are under half its footprint.
- The portfolio's lot count covers rental lots only, so it is not the full footprint.
- A person anchor is a display choice, not a named owner.

## Test cases

- Ordinary: a building in a multi-lot identity group.
- Difficult: a lot with several buildings; a co-op or condo exclusion; a deed-bridged group.

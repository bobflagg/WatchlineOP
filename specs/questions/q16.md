# Q16: Reports and filing documents

Existing local checks retain their stated query dates; they are not a frozen acceptance baseline. See [expected-answer policy](../roadmap.md#expected-answer-policy).

## Question

> What do published reports and retrieved filing documents say about this owner or entity, how do they relate to the recorded facts, and what is each one's review status?

## Who asks and why

News and filings answer "has there been notable news about my landlord".

## Required content of an answer

Inputs: entity or address; sources searched; as-of date; step and time budget.

For each item:
1. Publisher or filing body, date, URL or document id, and page.
2. The quoted claim or extracted value, and the extraction method.
3. `source_category` (`public_record`, `published_reporting`, `web_content`, `project_derived`) and `review_status` (`unreviewed`, `confirmed`, `refuted`, `inconclusive`, `contested`).
4. How it relates to the recorded facts: agrees, adds, conflicts, or unrelated.
5. The basis for treating it as about this entity (exact name or address, or a lead only).

## Acceptable incomplete

- Nothing found: "no reports or documents found in the sources searched", with the queries and dates. Never "no news" or "no issues".
- A document unreadable, partial or behind payment: an unresolved lead with the reason.
- An uncertain scanned name: reported with a low-confidence flag and not used as a link.

## Failures to avoid

- A claim stated as fact.
- Ownership inferred from a report or from a document's signatory.
- A person's name shown without its source and role.
- An entity matched on name alone without saying so.
- Contradicting reports resolved silently.
- A missing report read as nothing having happened.

## Test cases

- Ordinary: an article naming the exact LLC; a retrieved filing with a named manager.
- Difficult: an article naming a similarly named entity; two contradicting articles; a scanned attachment with an uncertain name; a paid-only registry step; a report dated after the as-of date.

## Caveats the answer must carry

A report is a claim that was published, not a fact. The record that it was published is recorded evidence. Whether the claim is true and applies is a separate, derived judgment. Web and document findings are session evidence, not graph facts.

## Scenarios

- A news article states that a fund controls the owner's parent company: shown as a reported claim, with publisher, date and quoted text, unreviewed, and kept apart from the Owner Portfolio.
- Two articles contradict each other: both shown, contested, neither chosen.
- A claim names a person whose recorded role in the filings differs: both returned, and the person shown only in the recorded role.
- A claim from before the as-of date is superseded by later records: reported with both dates.
- An article names an LLC with a similar but not identical name: reported as a lead about a different entity.
- A retrieved filing is partly unreadable: reported with the extraction method and a low-confidence flag.

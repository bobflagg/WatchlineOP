# Q15: Open violations of a building

## Question

> What open violations does a building have, by class, how long have they been open, and what is the rate per residential unit?

### Sub-questions

CLaRO-style cleanup (stage 1, 2026-11-18). Decision (user choice): violations are loaded for every HPD-registered lot, as `Violation` nodes linked to the building (BIN). Q15 passes when both pass.

| ID | Sub-question | Answer requirements covered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Q15.1 | Which violations does this building have, by class and status, and what is the oldest open inspection date? | 1 (counts by class), 2 (age), 4 (counting unit, status) |
| Q15.2 | What is the rate of open violations per residential unit of its lot? | 3 (rate), caveat on zero or missing units |
| Q15.3 | Which pest violations (mice, roaches, rodents, unspecified vermin) does this building have, by kind and status, and what is the oldest open one? | 1 (counts), 2 (age), 4 (counting unit, status); extension of 2026-11-21 |

### Hidden assumptions made explicit (profiled 2026-11-18)

| Assumption | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| "Registered lots/buildings" | A violation is loaded when its BBL is in HPD registrations and its BIN is a graph Building. 1,912,362 violations are on registered lots (471,058 open); 1,901,592 load; 10,770 are excluded and counted (3,540 with no usable BIN, the rest a BIN that is not a registered building) |
| "Open" | `violationstatus` 'Open' at the snapshot; 'Close' covers closed and dismissed. Finer HPD status is not stored |
| Building vs lot | Violations hang on the BIN. The unit count is the lot's PLUTO value; a BIN on several lots gets one rate per lot, never a sum |
| Apartment | Not stored |
| "Pest violation" (Q15.3, 2026-11-21) | A violation whose HPD order code (`ordernumber`) is listed in `data/hpd_pest_orders.yaml`. The graph stores only the code; the kind (mice 569 and 869, roaches 568 and 868, rodents 567 and 867, vermin-unspecified 566 and 866) is read from that list at answer time. 1,901,592 loaded violations all have a numeric code (364 distinct). The pest codes load 137,646 violations on registered buildings |
| "Mice" (Q15.3) | Only codes 569 and 869 say mice (100% of their descriptions). Codes 566 and 866 say "vermin" and cover mice, roaches, rats and others; they are reported as unspecified vermin, never as mice. Complaints do name the pest, in `problemcode` (Q27.3), but that is a tenant report, not an inspector's citation |

## Who asks and why

It is the main evidence of conditions an organizer can show a landlord or the city.

## Required content of an answer

1. Open violations by class (A, B, C, and I where present), with the snapshot date.
2. Age: the oldest open inspection date.
3. The rate per residential unit, with the numerator, the unit count, and the unit count's source.
4. The counting unit (violations) and the status used (open at the snapshot date).
5. For Q15.3: counts by pest kind and status with the oldest open inspection date, the order codes behind each kind, and the same caveats.

## Caveats the answer must carry

- A violation is what an inspector cited, not a measure of conditions.
- Open means open at the snapshot date.
- The rate uses PLUTO residential units, which are lot-wide.
- A zero or missing unit count gives an explicit "rate unavailable".
- A pest violation is an inspector's citation of an infestation, not a count of pests or of affected apartments. The kind comes from the order code; codes 566 and 866 do not say mice.

## Question

> Given an address, what is the traced dossier: title and registration facts, management relationship, Owner Portfolio, Operational Network, then any parent or control lead, each with a cited record, run and limits, and where does the trail stop?

This is scored as a whole, not as a single question. Its required components are Q1 to Q4, Q9, Q10, Q16 and Q19.

Hidden assumptions: the required component list in the question (Q1 to Q4, Q9, Q10, Q16, Q19) is incomplete. The recorded trail also needs Q5 (DOS), Q6 (masked and mail-drop addresses), Q7 (shared contacts), Q11 to Q13 (financing and deeds) and Q15 (violations); complaints and litigation are not loaded, so they are source gaps, not "none". A shared address is a record link, not common ownership. The stop reason is chosen from the five enumerated ones; a mail-drop list miss is not a clearance (the list is preliminary). Q20.4 and the investigation part of Q20.3 stay `pending` until a retrieval cache exists.

## Who asks and why

It is the whole research method as one document.

## Required content of an answer

1. The input address, resolution outcome, and identified lots and buildings.
2. Recorded title, registration, management and business-filing facts.
3. Operational Network and Owner Portfolio results, separately explained.
4. Transaction, financing, violation, complaint and litigation summaries.
5. Retrieved claims, document extractions and affiliation leads, with their labels.
6. Conflicts, source gaps, excluded evidence and unresolved leads.
7. Run and snapshot context, citations, retrieval log and an explicit stop reason.

## Acceptance requirements

1. **Lead rule.** A lead is a new entity name or address that appears in a recorded or retrieved source, is not masked as an aggregator or mail-drop address, and falls within the step and time budget. Everything else found is logged but not followed.
2. **Claim-to-source.** Every claim has a source record or URL, a retrieval time and a source category. No claim states ownership on the strength of a derived layer or a group's person anchor.
3. **Labels.** Each claim is a recorded assertion (Type I), a derived lead (Type II) or a reported claim, with its review status. A "hypothesis" is a derived or reported lead, never a finding.
4. **Unresolved leads.** Each lead not followed or not resolved is listed with its reason: masked address, no further record, source needs payment or is inaccessible, budget reached, or conflicting records.
5. **Stop reason.** Exactly one of: reached a registered-agent, mail-drop or masked address; no further recorded link; next source needs payment or is inaccessible; budget reached; conflicting records left unresolved.
6. **Other jurisdictions.** When a recorded entity is registered elsewhere, the agent searches that state's free public registry. A paid-only step is an unresolved lead with the reason "needs payment".
7. **Reproducibility.** The dossier records the run, the snapshot dates and everything retrieved, so the investigation can be replayed from the cache.

## Scenarios

- The trail ends at a registered-agent address after two entities: reported as the end, with the reason.
- The trail reaches a parent company only through a news claim: presented as a lead, and the dossier says the recorded part of the trail ended earlier.
- Permits (DOB) or program and subsidy lists are retrieved on demand: reported with their source and not added to the graph.

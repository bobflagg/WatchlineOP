# Q22: Same entity or not

## Question

> Where two records name entities differently (deed grantee vs HPD owner, or two registrations), what supports treating them as one entity, what argues against it, and what is left unresolved?

### Sub-questions

CLaRO-style cleanup (stage 1, 2026-11-19). No new graph element: contacts, grantees, grantors, document parties and DOS entities already carry `nameKey`. The comparison and the joining records are computed at answer time. The parent passes when both pass.

| ID | Sub-question | Answer requirements covered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Q22.1 | How do two recorded names compare: equal after which named rule, an abbreviation match, differing only by a numeral or word, or different? | 1, 2 (rule), 3 (numeral or word) |
| Q22.2 | Which recorded records name each entity, and which of them join the two (same lot, same registration, same document, same DOS id, same non-masked business address)? | 1 (sources), 2 and 3 (joining records), 4 (none found) |

Hidden assumptions: a name match never joins; only a non-name record does. Q3 and Q5 keep a space as a difference. Q22 adds its own rules (decision 2026-11-19): S1 spaces removed, L1 legal-suffix words (INCORPORATED, CORPORATION, COMPANY, DEVELOPMENT) to short form, AB1 abbreviation table (HDFC only). Rules apply in that order, after N3; the first that makes the names equal is reported. A DOS long form is found by Q22 only if its record is in the graph (scope: N3 key equals a graph name), so anchor D's DOS record is a documented gap until a lookup widens it.

## Who asks and why

It keeps an organizer from merging two companies, or splitting one, on spelling alone.

## Required content of an answer

1. The two names with their sources.
2. Evidence **for** treating them as one entity, with the rule used for any normalization.
3. Evidence **against**.
4. What is left unresolved.

## Caveats the answer must carry

- A normalization match is derived. A name match is a lead, not a link.
- Two names that differ by a numeral or a word (such as `Holdings` and `Holdings II`) are not the same entity unless a record other than the name connects them.
- Two names that differ only by punctuation, case or a legal suffix are reported as a normalization match, with the rule.
- If the only shared record is a masked or mail-drop address, it supports no identity.

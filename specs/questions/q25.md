## Question

> When a recorded entity is registered outside New York State, what do the free public registries of that jurisdiction show, and where does the search stop?

## Who asks and why

Landlords often hold buildings through entities registered in Delaware or New Jersey, where the New York record ends.

## Required content of an answer

1. The entity, the jurisdiction recorded for it, and which record said so.
2. The registry searched, the date, and what it returned: filing id, status, date, registered agent.
3. Any step that needs payment or an account, reported as an unresolved lead with the reason "needs payment".
4. Whether the entity found is the same one, and the basis.

## Caveats the answer must carry

- Registry findings are session evidence, not graph facts.
- Some registries show only a name and status. Officers and owners may not be public.
- A name match in another state is a lead until another record connects it.

## Scenarios

- A Delaware entity: the public search shows the name and status only; the next step needs payment, so it is an unresolved lead.
- A New Jersey entity with a public record: filing id and agent reported, with the retrieval date.
- A similarly named entity in another state: reported as a lead about a different entity.
- The registry cannot be reached: reported as such, with no inference.
